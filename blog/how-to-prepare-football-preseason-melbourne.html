How to prepare for football preseason in Melbourne

12 August 2026 4 min read APF Coaching Team

In Melbourne, the winter football season wraps up and a familiar temptation sets in. The training ground goes quiet. Recovery turns into weeks of doing nothing. Then, eight weeks before round one, every player tries to compress four months of work into six sessions and wonders why they are limping through the first block of matches. Preseason preparation is not what you do when the season is announced. It is the work you do in the weeks and months before preseason even starts.

Know what preseason actually demands

Preseason training in Melbourne typically runs eight to twelve weeks before round one. Most clubs at local and community level expect players to arrive at a base level of fitness. If you show up to the first session unable to hold an aerobic workload for forty minutes, you spend the first half of preseason catching up while others are building. The players who get the most from preseason are the ones who arrive already capable of holding that load. The goal is not to survive preseason - it is to use it.

Build your aerobic base before you need it

The biggest gap between a sharp preseason and a difficult one is aerobic capacity. You need a heart and lungs that can sustain repeated high-intensity efforts across a ninety-minute match. That does not happen in a week. For players who have access to open parks and ovals, a structured aerobic block through August and September sets the foundation everything else builds on.

Long, steady runs at a comfortable pace three times a week are a starting point, but footballers need more than base cardio. Interval work - short bursts at high effort followed by active recovery - trains your body to handle the energy demands of match play. A simple format: six sets of ninety seconds at near-maximum effort with ninety seconds of walking between. Repeat twice a week and you will see aerobic adaptation within four to six weeks.

Develop explosive power alongside your endurance

Football matches are decided in two-yard bursts. The acceleration out of a standing start, the explosive step to cut in front of a defender, the push off the ground to win a header. These are power outputs, and they are trainable. Bodyweight exercises like single-leg squats, step-ups, jump landings, and lateral bounds build the type of strength footballers actually use without needing a gym. Two sessions per week of this work alongside your aerobic block keeps the load manageable and the gains consistent.

What a solid eight-week prep block covers

  • Aerobic base: three steady-state sessions per week, building duration week on week
  • Intervals: two high-intensity sessions per week, keeping work periods short and effort high
  • Explosive work: two bodyweight strength sessions per week targeting lower-body power
  • Ball work: two to three skill sessions per week so technical quality does not drop off
  • Recovery: one full rest day and one light movement day per week, non-negotiable

Do not let your technical sharpness drop

Fitness is one side of preseason readiness. Technical quality is the other. Players who take the off-season completely away from the ball find their first touch hesitant, their passing heavy, and their decision-making slow in those opening rounds. Even two or three skill sessions per week through August and September keep your technical edge sharp. Passing against a rebounder, ball control in a tight space, or working on a specific movement pattern you want to improve for the coming season - none of it needs to be complicated to be effective.

How structured coaching changes your preseason

Working through a targeted off-season conditioning program with a private coach makes the preparation phase far more efficient. Our sessions are built around each player's specific needs - the aerobic gaps, the explosive weaknesses, the technical areas that dropped off through the season. Generic programs give you a starting point. A targeted program built around your position, your game, and your physical profile gives you the edge you are actually looking for.

The players we work with across Melbourne who use the off-season as a genuine build phase arrive at preseason at a different level. Not fitter in a vague sense - sharper in specific ways they can feel in the first session.

The players who arrive at preseason in shape are the ones who treated the off-season like it mattered. It does.
