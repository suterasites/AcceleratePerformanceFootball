Off-Season Is Where Next Season Is Won

9 September 2026 4 min read APF Coaching Team

The off-season in western Melbourne runs from around August through to preseason camps in late October. That is ten to twelve weeks. How a player uses that window - or wastes it - is often the single biggest factor separating where they start next season from where they finished this one.

Why the Gap Opens Up During the Break

Most players treat the off-season as a complete reset. They stop training, the body decompresses, and by the time preseason arrives they are spending the first three weeks just getting back to where they left off. That is not recovery - that is regression.

Aerobic fitness begins declining within two to three weeks of full inactivity. Touch and first-touch sharpness follow. The explosive speed and directional change built across a long season requires consistent stimulus to maintain. Without it, a player who trained hard all year can trade months of real development for a few weeks of rest they did not actually need.

The players who arrive at preseason in noticeably better condition did not get lucky. They made a deliberate choice to keep working when it was easy to stop.

What the Off-Season Is Actually For

The goal is not to grind through a second consecutive season. It is to address the things the competition calendar did not give you time or mental bandwidth to fix.

During the season, training is built around match preparation and recovery. There is limited room for new technical work or genuine physical development. The off-season removes that constraint entirely. A player can focus on weak areas without the pressure of an upcoming fixture: the left foot that never felt secure, the turning speed that cost them in tight areas, the aerobic base that faded in the back half of every game.

Used well, ten to twelve weeks is enough time to solve problems that dragged through an entire season and arrive somewhere different. Used poorly, it is the same problems starting again in February.

The Areas Worth Targeting

The specifics depend on the player and their position, but most off-season programs should cover some version of the following:

  • Aerobic endurance: building the conditioning base that carries you through 90 minutes without fading in the final third
  • Explosive power and acceleration: developing the first two steps that create separation and give you an edge in one-on-one situations
  • Agility and directional change: improving the ability to shift quickly and efficiently in tight spaces
  • Technical precision: passing accuracy, ball control, and first touch under low-pressure repetition, so the fundamentals become automatic at speed
  • Position-specific movement patterns: rehearsing the decisions and movement sequences your role demands, so game situations feel familiar rather than reactive

Why the Off-Season Is Different From In-Season Training

In-season sessions are time-pressured and shaped around keeping a player fresh for match day. Off-season work can go deeper. Loads can be higher. Drills can be more demanding. There is room to train something intensively enough that it actually sticks, rather than just maintaining it.

This is the window where a player can push into discomfort and recover fully before the next session, without worrying about whether tired legs will affect Saturday's performance. That freedom, used correctly, produces changes in fitness and technical ability that simply are not achievable during the playing calendar.

Why Private Training Makes a Difference in the Off-Season

Group preseason programs have their place, but they cannot address individual gaps. What a striker needs to work on at this point in the year is different from what a holding midfielder needs. A private off-season program can be built around exactly what that player's game is missing.

Off-season conditioning at APF starts with an assessment of where the player finished the season, what they want to look like at preseason, and what physical and technical work will close that distance fastest. We are Melbourne based and work with players across the city.

In a one-on-one setting, every repetition gets real feedback. There is no waiting, no generic drills that do not match your position, and no sessions where you coast because the coach is occupied with someone else. Over ten to twelve weeks, that level of individual attention produces visible, measurable results.

When to Start

The best time to begin off-season training is within the first week or two after the season ends. Leaving it two or three weeks means the early part of the window goes toward clawing back condition instead of building on it. In Melbourne's football calendar, that often means getting started now rather than waiting until the preseason push begins and the window has closed.

If you are a parent thinking about your junior's preparation, the same logic applies. The players who step into next season with noticeably better touch, fitness, and confidence are the ones who treated the off-season as training time rather than a break from it.
