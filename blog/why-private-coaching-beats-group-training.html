Why private coaching beats group training for fast improvement

5 August 2026 5 min read APF Coaching Team

In Melbourne's western suburbs, group training is the default for most footballers. You show up, do what everyone else does, and hope the reps transfer. Private coaching works differently. Instead of splitting a coach's attention across a full squad, every minute is built around your specific weaknesses, your position, and your goals. The difference in results is not subtle - players who make the switch routinely compress months of stagnation into weeks of measurable progress.

Why group training has a ceiling

Group sessions serve a purpose. They expose you to competitive pressure, build team chemistry, and give you time with the ball in a structured environment. But they carry an unavoidable structural limit: the coach is managing the group, not developing the individual. Instruction defaults to the average level of the squad. Strong players are underworked. Weaker players are overwhelmed. Nobody gets exactly what they need at the exact moment they need it.

The ceiling is most visible in technical training. A player with a persistent fault on the outside of their left foot will run the same passing drill as everyone else. They may do 20 repetitions and receive one general correction. In a private session, that fault becomes the focus of the entire session. Every rep is watched, the cause of the error is identified, and the adjustment is made until the pattern starts to change. Faster improvement is not a promise - it is the structural consequence of a tighter feedback loop.

The feedback loop at the centre of improvement

Football development depends on feedback loops. You try something, you get feedback, you adjust. In a group session, that loop is slow - you might wait ten minutes between attempts at the same skill, and the coaching input you receive is general rather than targeted. In a private session, the loop is tight. Immediate, specific feedback after each rep. That compression of the feedback cycle is why private coaching produces faster improvement and why the improvements are more durable - they are built on correction, not repetition of a faulty pattern.

There is also an honesty element to private coaching that group training cannot replicate. In a group, players measure themselves against teammates and can quietly avoid their weaknesses. One-on-one, nothing is hidden. We assess where a player actually sits at the start of each session, and the training targets the gap between where they are and where they need to be.

What this looks like in practice

At our sessions at West Point Soccer Club in Point Cook, we start each engagement by identifying the specific things a player wants to improve. A midfielder from Werribee working on decision-making under pressure gets a session built entirely around that. We do not run a generic warm-up circuit. We warm up appropriately, move to the focal skill quickly, and layer in pressure - defensive, time, and positional - as the session progresses.

The plan is built around position, season timing, and the specific skills limiting performance. An attacker from Altona who struggles to get shots away quickly gets different work than a centre-back from Williamstown who needs to improve their passing range. Neither of those plans exists in a group session because the group can only do one thing at a time.

The compounding effect

Private coaching compounds. The first session produces a technical adjustment. The second reinforces that adjustment and adds a new layer. Over a 10 to 13 week block, the changes stack in a way that group training cannot replicate, because group training cannot pick up precisely where the last session left off for a specific individual. Progress is tracked, the plan is adjusted, and the next session builds on what was achieved in the previous one.

The 92% progression rate we see across 125+ athletes trained at APF is the result of exactly this process. Every training block is built around what an individual actually needs, and the plan is updated weekly based on what we observe in sessions. That kind of precise tracking is structurally impossible in a group setting.

When group training still makes sense

Private coaching is not the answer to everything. Group training is essential for match fitness, team dynamics, and playing under competitive pressure alongside others. The players who get the best results from private coaching are those who use it alongside their club sessions, not instead of them. Private sessions sharpen the technical tools. Club and competition fixtures put those tools under real match pressure. Both are needed - but they serve different functions.

Comparing your options

  • Private 1-on-1: Maximum coaching attention. Every correction is for you, every rep is observed, every session plan is built around your specific weaknesses and goals
  • Small Unit (2 to 6 players): Train with a teammate or two while still receiving individually targeted feedback - not coaching averaged across a group
  • Club group training: Best for competitive exposure, team chemistry, and match fitness - not for fixing technical faults at the root
  • Combining both: The players from Hoppers Crossing, Werribee, Point Cook and across the western suburbs who improve fastest use private sessions to sharpen technique and club sessions to apply it under pressure
  • Neither replaces a match: Real games expose gaps that training cannot always predict - but private coaching gives you the tools to close those gaps between fixtures

Where to start if you are serious about improving

If you have been stuck at the same level for a season or more, group training alone will not change that. It maintains your current level. Private coaching raises it. The Senior Refinement program at APF starts with an honest assessment of where you sit - technically, physically, and tactically - then builds a plan with clear targets and weekly tracking so progress is measured, not guessed.

Group training is where you maintain your level. Private coaching is where you raise it. The gap between the two is not small.
